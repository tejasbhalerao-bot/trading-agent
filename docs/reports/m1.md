# M1 Report: Design of Decide, Record and Learn

**Finished:** 2026-10-11. **Awaiting your decisions** (below).

## The project, in two lines

A system that studies the Indian share market each evening and suggests trades for the next one to three days. It never places an order, it runs on fake money first, and it keeps a permanent record of every decision so it can learn from its own results. Full description: [spec.md](../spec.md).

## What M1 was for

To design the three jobs the whole project depends on:
- **Decide**, which chooses the trades;
- **Record**, which writes every decision into a notebook that is never edited;
- **Learn**, which reads the finished notebook and adjusts how much each checklist is trusted.

M1 is design only; no code. The full design is in [design/pass1-decide-record-learn.md](../design/pass1-decide-record-learn.md).

## Words used here

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Checklist | A named set of yes/no conditions a share must all meet before it is considered, plus where its stop and target go. |
| Stop / target | The price to sell at if the trade goes wrong / right. Fixed before buying. |
| Market mood | Up, down or sideways. |
| Cell | One checklist in one market mood. Trust is tracked per cell. |
| Paper trading | Practising with fake money on real prices. |
| Backtest | Running the checklists over past years to see how they would have done. |

## The design in brief

- **Every exit is fixed when the trade is bought:** stop, target, or sell after three trading days. Nothing is moved later.
- **Buys happen at the next day's open.** A trade that opens beyond its own stop or target is cancelled.
- **Learning decides *what* to buy, never *how much*.** Size comes only from your safety limits.
- **Confidence means one checkable thing:** the estimated chance the trade makes money after costs. Learn regularly checks whether stated confidence matched what happened.
- **During paper trading, a checklist stays in use until the evidence shows it loses money.** Fake money costs nothing, and trading is the only way to learn.
- **Claude writes the case for each trade and may object, giving a reason from a fixed list.** Code checks its review. A bad or missing review is simply set aside.
- **Every number Learn produces lists the notebook entries behind it.**

**An honest limit.** Three to four months of paper trading gives roughly 60–120 trades. That is enough to show whether confidence is honest to within about ±10 percentage points, not finer. The end-of-paper report will say so plainly, and recommend more paper time if the evidence is too thin.

## Decisions for you

### 1. Safety limits

| Limit | Recommended | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Paper starting balance | **The amount you would really trade with** (₹10 lakh if unsure) | Using your real amount makes trade sizes realistic. |
| Risk per trade | **1% of the balance** | 0.5% gives smaller swings but slower results. 2% means ten losses in a row cost about 18%. |
| Max open positions | **5** | Fewer means fewer trades and less evidence. More means more exposure to one bad market day. |
| Max position size | **20% of the balance** | Stops a very tight stop from putting too much money in one share. |
| Max total open risk | **5% of the balance** | The most lost if every open stop is hit at once. Matches 5 positions × 1%. |
| Losing-run brake | **10% below the highest balance: halve the risk. 15% below: pause new buys for 10 trading days, then resume at half risk until back within 10%.** | Tighter brakes protect more but trade less, which means less evidence. Looser brakes let a losing run go further. |

### 2. How confidence is worked out

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The cell's smoothed win rate (recommended)** | Explainable in one sentence. Works with few trades: thin cells borrow from their checklist's overall record. | Every trade in the same cell gets the same confidence. |
| B. A scoring model using each candidate's figures | Can tell two trades in the same cell apart. | Needs far more trades than paper trading will give. Harder to explain. Likely to fit noise. |
| C. Set by hand per checklist | Simplest. | No learning, which the spec requires. |

### 3. Claude's veto

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Recorded, not obeyed, during paper trading (recommended)** | The trade is taken anyway, so the record shows whether Claude's objections were right. The veto earns power only if they were. This is how the spec says the veto must be granted. | No protection from Claude's judgment during paper trading. The money is fake, so nothing is lost. |
| B. Obeyed from day one | Simpler. | Unproven, and vetoed trades leave no evidence about whether the veto helps. |
| C. No veto; Claude writes the case only | Simplest. | Gives up a possible safeguard against news the rules cannot see. |

### 4. Run order (a spec change)
The spec lists the jobs in the order one decision lives through them. Each evening's run must do Settle and Learn **before** Decide, otherwise Decide would count positions already stopped out that day. No job's responsibility changes. **Recommendation: approve.**

### 5. Cost estimate (check)
Results are judged after an estimated **0.25% per round trip**: transaction tax, stamp duty and exchange fees, at a broker with no brokerage on delivery trades. If your broker charges brokerage on delivery, tell me the rate.
